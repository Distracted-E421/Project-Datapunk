In today’s column, I examine the “name your feelings” form of emotional therapy that is
sweeping widely and has become a pervasive trend. The interest here is that generative AI
and large language models (LLMs) can be instrumental in people pursuing this fascinating
cognitive act of naming and then reflecting on their personal feelings.

Is the use of AI for such a purpose an altogether good thing that aids people and society in
general, or might it have gotchas and downsides that could undermine our mental health?

Name Your Feelings Is Something Real

In case you are unaware of the “name your feelings” phenomenon, all you need to do is
know about the blockbuster movie Inside Out which portrayed animated characters
representing a set of feelings, including a sense of joy, sadness, disgust, fear, and anger.
The sequel Inside Out 2 carried forward that concept and added a sense of self, along with a
few additional emotions tossed into the mix for further measure.

The gist is this.

According to the “name your feelings” precepts, you are supposed to mindfully reflect on
your feelings, aiming to identify them and then mull them over. Some experts refer to this as
a form of emotional processing therapy. In a manner of speaking, you are encouraged or
given direct permission to surface your feelings and decide what to do about them.

Theory suggests that by surfacing your feelings as a subject of analysis, you have a much
better chance of managing those feelings. People tend to bury their feelings within them and
not become avidly aware of their feelings. The feelings often overtake the rest of their
thinking and actions. You can see this at times when you observe someone acting out and
they appear to be oblivious to the fact that their feelings have turned them into feelings-
driven reactive creatures.

Part of the reason that naming your feelings has become a widespread trend is that it is quite
easy to do. By and large, you are to first become self-aware about your feelings. Second,
you are to name or give labels to the feelings. That’s how you make them explicit. Third, you
are to analyze the identified feelings and ascertain where they come from, and what impact
they are having on you, including otherwise assessing the feelings. Fourth, once you’ve
taken those steps, you are hopefully ready to begin to manage those feelings and proceed
on a better course in life.

Voila, you are on the path to better mental health and life prosperity.

Self-Analysis Of Feelings Is Challenging

Like most things in life, the reality of “name your feelings” is not as easy as it seems.

2/7

You might not be able to figure out what your feelings are. Thus, you can’t get past the first
step and proceed to the subsequent steps. Or, possibly even worse, you might misidentify
your feelings. If you decide that you are feeling disgusted when the true feeling is more like
anger, you have made a misdiagnosis and will presumably be on a misdirected path. Sad
face.

Suppose you nail down your feelings and are sure of what they are. Analyzing the root
causes and trying to ferret out the basis for the feelings is a whole additional can of worms.
You could inadvertently pin the blame on false assumptions. There is a chance too that you
will berate yourself for things that aren’t pertinent to the feelings at hand. And so on.

That’s why using online guides or watching YouTube videos to figure out the proper methods
to undertake “name your feelings” can be unfulfilling. The weak link of sorts is that you are
doing this entirely on your own.

Can you really do a reasonable job of the arduous task?

Naturally, the use of a mental health professional can make a huge difference in how things
turn out. A versed professional can walk you through the entire process. They can gauge
whether you are doing a true introspection or trying to fake the matter. When it comes to
analyzing your feelings, having someone else that you can bounce things off is great,
especially via a trained therapist who also will respect your privacy on these highly personal
revelations.

Not everyone can afford a therapist. Those who can afford a therapist might not have the
time to logistically see the therapist. A slew of barriers to therapy are simply due to cost,
time, and other commonplace factors. Well, it turns out that there is a relatively new means
of getting a kind of therapeutic counseling on “name your feelings” that is nearly free to use,
can be accessed 24 x 7, and doesn’t require an appointment or similar hassles.

What’s that, you might be asking with bated breath.

The said-to-be answer is that you can make use of generative AI and large language models
to aid the “name your feelings” endeavor.

Generative AI Enters Into The Scene

Given the incredible fluency of modern-day generative AI, people have been leaning into the
likes of ChatGPT, GPT-4o, o1, Claude, Gemini, Llama, and many other generic AI apps for
their dose of mental health advisement. This can be done daily, weekly, or on any schedule
you prefer. All you need to do is sign up for an account on these generative AI apps and start
a dialogue with the AI. Period, end of story.

Example Of How Generative AI Fits In

I’m betting you’d like to see what a “name your feelings” experience would be like if you
made use of generative AI.

Great, let’s go ahead and see an example. I logged into a popular generative AI app and
pretended that I needed a “name your feelings” session or conversation. All you need to do
is tell the AI in a prompt that you want to carry out that type of discussion. It is easy-peasy to
initiate. No arcane commands are needed, and you don’t have to browbeat the AI to
proceed.

Here we go.

User entered prompt: “I’m kind of off today. There’s just a lot to consider, such as my
work, my family, career, mortgage, car payments, and a lot more.”
Generative AI response: “Is there a specific feeling that comes to mind?”
User entered prompt: “Well, I’m not sure what specific feeling there is, but I know for
sure that I am completely overwhelmed. Sometimes I doubt that I can keep up with
everything that is going on.”
Generative AI response: “Perhaps what you are feeling is a form of apprehension and
even a tinge of hopelessness. Does that seem to fit your existing circumstances?”
User entered prompt: “Yes, that’s it, thanks. Naming the emotions helps me to
understand what’s going on. It will make my life much more manageable.”
Generative AI response: “You are welcome. Identifying emotions can often bring
clarity. Let me know if you need help working through those feelings.”

I kept the conversation short since I just wanted to provide an illustrative example for you.

In actual practice, you would undoubtedly engage in a lengthy dialogue with AI. There would
be an extensive back-and-forth while working through the process. You can proceed at your
own pace. Ergo, you can start the conversation, pause to do something else and come back
to it later.

Getting AI To Be Emotional

If you look closely at the dialogue, you’ll see that the AI was responding in a somewhat
clinical manner. The AI was proceeding on a dry basis that was relatively matter-of-fact.

I’ve got a surprise for you.

Most people don’t realize that you can tell generative AI to essentially fake or pretend to
exhibit emotions and feelings. I’ve explored this facet in-depth, see the link here and the link
here. The crux is that though the AI isn’t experiencing emotions and feelings, how it
responds to you can have an appearance of those aspects.

I will illustrate this. I went ahead and started a new conversation with the AI. I also told it in a
prompt that it should exhibit emotions or feelings during the discussion.

Here’s what happened.

User entered prompt: “I’m kind of off today. There’s just a lot to consider, such as my
work, my family, career, mortgage, car payments, and a lot more.”

Your Feelings About This Usage Of AI

Allowing generative AI to compose responses as though it is a human or akin to humans is a
serious matter.

A concerted phrase used in the AI field is that this is a form of anthropomorphizing AI.
People will potentially fall into a mental trap of believing that AI is sentient. That’s a falsehood
and could be endangering to people who land in that morass.

There are other notable concerns about using AI to aid you in a “name your feelings”
exercise. Suppose the AI tells you that to deal with your feelings, you need to jump up and
down and run around in circles. I say this because there is no guarantee that the AI won’t
say something untoward and cause a person to be misguided or misdirected. The advice by
the AI might be entirely wrong.

Another qualm consists of privacy considerations. People seem to think that when using
generative AI, everything they enter is completely private. Nope. Sorry to say that this isn’t
likely. Most of the AI makers state in their licensing agreements that they reserve the right to
